# Quarantine and Material Handling

## Red tags and the quarantine cage
Every non-conforming part gets a red tag carrying its NCR number and is moved to the
locked quarantine cage Q1 beside the inspection bench. The shift supervisor holds the
key. Nothing leaves the cage without a signed disposition.

## Material lot traceability
Each tote carries the lot number from the bar stock material certificate. When a crack
is found, every part machined from the same material lot is held until the lab has
checked a sample from that lot. This is the lot review that Rule R1 asks for.

## Preventing handling damage
Housings travel in foam-lined totes, at most twelve per tote, with no metal-on-metal
stacking. Wear nitrile gloves when moving finished parts. A scratch caused by handling
is still judged under Rule R2 like any other surface scratch.

## Releasing parts from quarantine
A part leaves quarantine only after its NCR disposition is signed. Replace the red tag
with a green tag, record the release in the quarantine log, and return the part to the
operation named in the disposition.

## Untagged parts
A part found on the floor or in a tote without a tag or traveler is treated as
non-conforming until its work order is confirmed. Put it in the quarantine cage and
tell the shift supervisor.
