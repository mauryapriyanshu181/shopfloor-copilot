# Injection Moulding Defect Guide (Press IM-02)

## Scope
Applies to injection-moulded covers produced on press IM-02. Defects on machined CNC
housings are covered by the inspection SOP (SOP-QA-014) and the rework-vs-scrap rules,
not by this guide.

## Short shot
The cavity did not fill completely, leaving an unfilled edge on the cover. Typical causes
are low injection pressure, a cold mould or a blocked vent. A short-shot cover cannot be
reworked. Scrap it and ask the press operator to check melt temperature and vents.

## Flash
Flash is a thin fin of excess plastic along the parting line where the mould halves meet.
Flash under 0.3 mm can be trimmed by hand with a deburring blade at the press. Flash over
0.3 mm, or any flash on the sealing lip, means the mould is worn: quarantine the batch
and call maintenance.

## Sink marks
Sink marks are shallow depressions that form over thick sections when the resin cools
unevenly. On the outer face they are acceptable if they cannot be seen at arm's length
under the inspection lamp. Any sink mark on the sealing lip is rejected.

## Stress cracks and knit lines
A knit line is the faint seam where two flow fronts meet. It is acceptable if it cannot be
felt with a fingernail. A stress crack is different: it is a real fracture, usually
starting at a screw boss. A cracked cover is treated like any cracked part and scrapped
under Rule R1. Regrinding cracked covers back into the resin supply is not permitted.

## Warpage
A warped cover does not sit flat. Check it on the surface plate with a feeler gauge: a
gap above 0.5 mm at any corner is a reject. Warped covers must not be heat-straightened
or re-pressed; scrap them and record the cooling time used on the press.
